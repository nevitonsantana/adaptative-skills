# Product method selection

Use this reference before applying a prioritization method. A method is a decision aid, not an authority.

| Decision shape | Prefer first | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Fast comparison with limited data | ICE or value versus effort | Scores would be treated as precise forecasts |
| Comparable initiatives with reach and effort estimates | RICE | Reach or impact are invented or incomparable |
| Customer satisfaction trade-offs | Kano | No customer evidence or requirement classification exists |
| Time-sensitive economic sequencing | Cost of delay | Delay cost and duration cannot be estimated credibly |
| Outcome or unmet-need opportunity ranking | Opportunity scoring | The underlying outcome and user context are unclear |

## Selection rules

1. Fix the decision object before choosing the method.
2. Prefer the least complex method that can distinguish the candidates.
3. Reject a method when its critical inputs are fabricated, incomparable, or unavailable.
4. Keep the reasoning behind scores and record the largest uncertainty.
5. Use a qualitative decision record when arithmetic would create false precision.
6. Use one primary method and at most one alternative in a single review.
